Markup Calculator

Calculate a product's selling price using its unit cost and markup percentage. See your markup amount and estimated gross profit margin before setting a price.

What Is Markup?

Markup is the amount added to the cost of a product to determine its selling price. Businesses use markup to help set prices and plan for operating expenses and profit.

Markup Formula

The markup percentage is applied to the original unit cost.

Markup Amount = Unit Cost × Markup Percentage ÷ 100

Selling Price = Unit Cost + Markup Amount

Markup Calculator Example

Suppose a product costs $25 and you apply a 40% markup.

  • Unit cost: $25
  • Markup percentage: 40%
  • Markup amount: $10
  • Selling price: $35
  • Gross profit margin: 28.57%

Markup vs. Profit Margin

Markup and profit margin are related but different measurements. Markup compares profit with the original cost, while profit margin compares profit with the selling price.

A 40% markup does not mean a 40% profit margin. For example, a product costing $25 and selling for $35 has a 40% markup but a gross profit margin of approximately 28.57%.

What to Consider When Setting Prices

  • Include all relevant product and purchasing costs.
  • Consider shipping, packaging and transaction fees.
  • Compare prices with similar products in your market.
  • Review customer demand and your business expenses.
  • Remember that taxes and operating expenses may reduce net profit.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate a 40% markup?

Multiply the unit cost by 0.40 to find the markup amount, then add that amount to the original cost.

Is markup the same as profit margin?

No. Markup is based on cost, while profit margin is based on the selling price.

Can I use this calculator for different currencies?

Yes. Select USD, CAD, GBP, AUD or EUR. The calculator uses your selected currency to display the results.

Note: This calculator provides estimates for general business planning. It does not include taxes, operating expenses or other additional costs unless you include them in the unit cost. Check important pricing decisions with a qualified professional.
